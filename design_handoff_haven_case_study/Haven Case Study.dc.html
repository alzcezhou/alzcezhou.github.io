01 CONTEXT

Going to the police or the school often feels worse than doing nothing. What if students could warn each other — and find help — without going through institutions?

02 RESEARCH · 100-STUDENT ANONYMOUS SURVEY
64.6%

said lack of anonymity kept them from reporting

90.6%

of those would report if it were anonymous

96.5%

said multiple anonymous reports would change where they go

Survey findings became 3 personas — and the core of our pitch.

03 KEY DECISIONS

One app, two modes, one toggle

1Reduce Harm — nearby clinics and services on one map
2Report Harm — anonymous incidents pinned to places
WHY
Reporting and getting help usually live in separate systems. A single toggle bridges them while keeping each mode focused.

Anonymity from the first screen

Problem → fear of being identified was the #1 barrier.
Decision → verify with a university email; collect nothing identifying.
Outcome → trust is set before any sensitive content is asked for.

Crowdsourced trust signals

Problem → institutional directories lack peer trust.
Decision → map-based resources with crowdsourced ratings and services at a glance.
Outcome → students judge a provider's safety fast, in high-stress moments.
